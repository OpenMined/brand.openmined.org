**Rule.** Where color carries information, violet and blue never have to be told apart.

- **Charts.** Two series next to each other are never violet and blue. The chart series order already keeps them apart; a chart that picks its own colors keeps them apart too.
- **Comparisons.** Violet and blue never mark the two sides of a comparison (before/after, A/B, kept/lost), or any two things a reader has to tell apart, in a chart, diagram, or interface.

**Doesn't apply: color as a flourish.** The rule covers color used as a label, where a reader decodes the hue. Where color is decoration and carries no meaning of its own, violet and blue may sit side by side:

- **Spectrums.** A run of hues read as one spectrum, such as a diagram's source groups running red, violet, blue, teal, green.
- **Two-tone gradients.** Blue's companion is violet: one color shifting in motion.

**Why.** Under the most common color blindness (deuteranopia) the pair is nearly indistinguishable. That costs nothing when the two carry one meaning, and loses information when they carry two.
