title: If color, then motion
description: Color used for expression moves while it's on screen, or arrives with an interaction. Color that carries meaning is exempt, and so is the logo. A working rule to test, not a settled one.
status: released
date: 2026-10-12
tags: [color, graphics, interface]
---

**Context.** Color used to break up content makes an impression loud: too many colors in one view. On Oct 9 the answer was color as an interactive layer: an infographic's hovered section turns color while the rest stays gray. That left static impression sections, such as the pillar grid on openmined.org/research, which have nothing to interact with but still want some life and color. The pillar diamonds on that page, filled with a slowly flowing spectrum and a glow that flows with it, were the example in the Oct 12 review, where the brand owner proposed the rule: *if color, then motion.*

**Decision.** Wherever there is color, there is also motion: either persistent motion while the color is on screen (an ambient loop, like the diamonds) or color that arrives with an interaction (a hover, an expanded accordion, the current step of an infographic). Which elements get color stays a design call, made by a person for now. The rule only says that color, once used, moves. Exempt: color that carries meaning (an error's red, a status, a chart series) unless we decide otherwise for a given case, and the logo. Under reduced motion the color stays and the motion stops.

**Alternatives.** Color only as an interactive layer (Oct 9), which leaves static impression sections with no color. No rule, judging each piece on its own, which is how the kaleidoscope look happens.

**Consequences.** A one-time entrance animation doesn't count as motion; the motion has to persist. The guardrails (how much color and movement one view or one piece may carry) are not set yet. *Color moves* lists the leanings and the open questions, and exploration rounds come before any of them become rules.

**Revisit when.** The exploration rounds (icon color modes, the research diamonds in v2 colors, button hover motion, SyftHub's two halves, colorflow along the ABC diagram lines) show where the rule holds and where it doesn't.
